**Fauda** (Arabic: فوضى, also known as **FAUDA** and the **Fauda
Movement for Palestine**) is an
<a href="Anarchism" class="wikilink" title="anarchist">anarchist</a>
organisation active in the
<a href="Palestinian_territories" class="wikilink"
title="Palestinian territories">Palestinian territories</a>. Formed in
the <a href="West_Bank" class="wikilink" title="West Bank">West Bank</a>
in 2020, it has been described as Palestine's first and largest
anarchist organisation.[^1]

## History

### Origins

<a href="Anarchism" class="wikilink" title="Anarchism">Anarchism</a>
remained largely unknown within the Palestinian territories for most of
modern history. Anarchist ideas only began gaining traction after
several Palestinian students encountered the tradition while studying in
the United States and Britain.[^2] The 2020
<a href="George_Floyd" class="wikilink" title="George Floyd">George
Floyd</a> uprising in the US, and the visibility of
<a href="Anarchism_in_the_United_States" class="wikilink"
title="American anarchists">American anarchists</a> within it, was a
direct inspiration for these students forming their own
organisation.[^3] They named it Fauda — Arabic for 'anarchy'.

Fauda draws members from Muslim, Christian, and Jewish backgrounds.[^4]

### Allegations of non-existence

The Russian anarchist website *avtonom.org* questioned Fauda's
existence, citing the scarcity of images documenting their activities.
The site raised the possibility that Fauda was a fabrication — perhaps
by people outside Palestine, or even by Israeli intelligence — designed
to surveil Western and Russian leftists who made contact.[^5]

These allegations were subsequently refuted. The anarchist Instagram
page @abolishtheusa published images of Fauda members putting up posters
in the Palestinian territories and distributing food in the
<a href="Al-Aqsa_Mosque_compound" class="wikilink"
title="Al-Aqsa Mosque compound">Al-Aqsa Mosque compound</a>.[^6] The
Israeli anarchist organisation *anarchyin48* also affirmed Fauda's
existence, noting that some anti-Zionist Jews are members.[^7]

### Fundraising controversy

In November 2023, a Yale University student dance company, Ballet
Folklórico Mexicano de Yale, projected a QR code on stage during two
performances. The code linked to an Instagram post containing a Venmo
fundraising link for Fauda.[^8] The company's four co-presidents later
apologised for including the link.[^9]

## Organisation

Fauda operates through four units, each focused on a distinct area of
activity:[^10]

- **Education unit** — introduces Palestinians to anarchist theory and
  tactics.
- **Executive unit** — organises vigils and protests, including several
  held outside Israeli courts and police stations in the West Bank.[^11]
- **News unit** — reports on the Israeli-Palestinian conflict.
- **Media unit** — documents Palestinian history to counter the
  narrative promoted by Israeli state media.

The collective is structured without formal leadership, operating as a
leaderless movement.[^12] By 2024 it had convened approximately 130
members for its summer school.[^13]

Members visit the families of imprisoned Palestinians in the West
Bank[^14] and distribute food and water during *iftar* and *suhur*.[^15]

## Political Position

### The future of Palestine

Fauda advocates for the abolition of all states. In the current
circumstances, the group considers a one-state solution between the
River Jordan and the Mediterranean Sea the most achievable immediate
goal, with a subsequent transition toward a stateless society as the
longer-term aim.[^16]

Fauda is sharply critical of workers' states and Marxist approaches to
power:

> Focusing excessively on laws and the management of society by the
> state will lead to excessive accumulation of power, as we have seen in
> many <a href="Marxism" class="wikilink" title="Marxist">Marxist</a>
> movements, and this will increase the possibilities of corruption and
> tyranny.

The group rejects Mahmoud Abbas and the Palestinian Authority
outright.[^17]

### Relationship with other Palestinian groups

Fauda calls for unity among all Palestinian groups against
<a href="Zionism" class="wikilink" title="Zionism">Zionism</a>,
regardless of ideological difference.[^18] On Hamas, Fauda has described
it as a 'purely Islamic group' in contrast to Fauda's multi-faith
membership, while maintaining that both share the goal of fighting
Zionism.[^19]
