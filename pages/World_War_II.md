# World War Two

**World War II** (often abbreviated to **WWII** or **WW2**), also known
as the **Second World War**, was the largest war in history, fought
between 1939 and 1945 and killing between 50 and 85 million people. The
war was fought between two factions, the Allies and the Axis. It
dramatically reshaped the balance of power among
<a href="State_(Polity)" class="wikilink" title="states">states</a>,
with the geopolitical power of Western Europe rapidly falling and the
emergence of two new <a href="Superpower_(Geopolitics)" class="wikilink"
title="superpowers">superpowers</a> in the
<a href="United_States" class="wikilink" title="United States">United
States</a> and the
<a href="Soviet_Union" class="wikilink" title="Soviet Union">Soviet
Union</a>. The collapse of the European empires opened the way for a new
wave of anti-colonial movements across the world, while the
<a href="Anti-Fascism" class="wikilink"
title="anti-fascist">anti-fascist</a> coalition that had won the war in
Europe provided the political conditions under which
<a href="Social_Democracy" class="wikilink"
title="social democracy">social democracy</a> consolidated across much
of Western Europe.

For anarchists the war presents a difficult inheritance. The defeat of
fascism was a victory whose human cost was unimaginable, achieved by
ordinary people fighting and dying in numbers that defy comprehension.
But the war was won by an alliance of states — the
<a href="British_Empire" class="wikilink" title="British Empire">British
Empire</a>, the
<a href="United_States" class="wikilink" title="United States">United
States</a>, the <a href="USSR" class="wikilink" title="USSR">USSR</a> —
whose own records included colonialism, segregation, and mass political
violence; and the post-war settlement entrenched the very state and
military structures anarchists had long warned would produce the next
catastrophe.

## History of the War

### Background

The seeds of World War II were sown by the impact of
<a href="World_War_I" class="wikilink" title="World War I">World War
I</a> and the <a href="Great_Depression" class="wikilink"
title="Great Depression">Great Depression</a>, which led many countries
to slide into authoritarianism and political extremism. The
<a href="February_Revolution_(Russia)" class="wikilink"
title="Russian Empire collapsed">Russian Empire collapsed</a> and was
replaced by the USSR; the <a href="Weimar_Republic" class="wikilink"
title="Weimar Republic">Weimar Republic</a> was seized by
<a href="Adolf_Hitler" class="wikilink" title="Hitler">Hitler</a> and
turned into
<a href="Nazi_Germany" class="wikilink" title="Nazi Germany">Nazi
Germany</a>; and <a href="Benito_Mussolini" class="wikilink"
title="Mussolini">Mussolini</a> led the March on Rome and created a
fascist state in Italy. Several other
<a href="Revolutions_of_1927_-_1936" class="wikilink"
title="fascist and leftist uprisings">fascist and leftist uprisings</a>
across Europe — notably in
<a href="February_Uprising_(Austria)" class="wikilink"
title="Austria">Austria</a>, Spain, Portugal and Hungary — produced a
continent of mutually hostile and militarised governments.

The Great Depression and the steady erosion of parliamentary politics in
<a href="Japanese_Empire" class="wikilink" title="Japan">Japan</a>
concentrated power in the military. Cut off from global trade by tariffs
and drying international credit, Japan formed an alliance with Nazi
Germany and Fascist Italy and began to conquer surrounding territories
for resources. Japan invaded Manchuria in 1931 — destroying among other
things the <a href="Shinmin_Autonomous_Zone" class="wikilink"
title="Shinmin Zone">Shinmin Zone</a>, a Korean anarchist federation —
and Italy invaded Ethiopia in 1935. Nazi Germany annexed Austria and
dismembered Czechoslovakia in 1938 with the acquiescence of the Western
powers at
<a href="Munich_Agreement" class="wikilink" title="Munich">Munich</a>.

All the Axis states pursued a doctrine of imperial self-sufficiency.
Japan planned a Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere of puppet states
across Asia and the Pacific. Italy aimed to rebuild a
<a href="Roman_Empire" class="wikilink" title="Roman Empire">Roman
Empire</a> across Southern Europe and Northern Africa. Germany pursued a
<a href="New_Order_(Nazism)" class="wikilink" title="New Order">New
Order</a> of racial hierarchy and
*<a href="Lebensraum" class="wikilink" title="Lebensraum">Lebensraum</a>*
in the East.

The <a href="Spanish_Civil_War" class="wikilink"
title="Spanish Civil War">Spanish Civil War</a> (1936–1939) was a
rehearsal for the wider conflict. Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy
supplied troops, aircraft, and weapons to
<a href="Francisco_Franco" class="wikilink"
title="Franco&#39;s">Franco's</a> Nationalists. The Western democracies
imposed a non-intervention policy that materially favoured the fascists.
The USSR sent advisers and arms to the Republican side, but used its
influence to suppress the <a href="Spanish_Revolution" class="wikilink"
title="anarchist and POUM revolution">anarchist and POUM revolution</a>
within Republican territory,
<a href="Stalinism_vs_Anarchism_in_the_Spanish_Civil_War"
class="wikilink"
title="prioritising the destruction of revolutionary forces">prioritising
the destruction of revolutionary forces</a> over the defeat of Franco.
The fall of the Republic in early 1939 left fascism unchallenged in
Iberia for nearly four decades and demonstrated the willingness of all
the major powers to sacrifice anti-fascist movements to their own
strategic interests.

### 1937

One of the proposed dates for the beginning of the war is the start of
the Second Sino-Japanese War in July 1937. Japan attempted to take
control of China but became bogged down in a long and brutal conflict
that would last until 1945. The war was conducted with extraordinary
cruelty on both sides; the <a href="Nanjing_Massacre" class="wikilink"
title="Nanjing Massacre">Nanjing Massacre</a> of December 1937, in which
Japanese troops killed an estimated 200,000 to 300,000 Chinese civilians
and prisoners over six weeks, remains one of the worst atrocities of the
twentieth century.

### 1938

In March, Nazi Germany annexed Austria in the
*<a href="Anschluss" class="wikilink" title="Anschluss">Anschluss</a>*,
met with no resistance from the Western powers. In September, the
<a href="Munich_Agreement" class="wikilink"
title="Munich Agreement">Munich Agreement</a> handed the
<a href="Sudetenland" class="wikilink"
title="Sudetenland">Sudetenland</a> of Czechoslovakia to Germany, with
Britain and France agreeing in the absence of the Czechoslovak
government and against the protest of the USSR. The agreement was widely
understood at the time as a triumph for the avoidance of war; in
retrospect it confirmed to Hitler that further aggression would not be
resisted. *<a href="Kristallnacht" class="wikilink"
title="Kristallnacht">Kristallnacht</a>* followed in November, marking
the open turn of Nazi anti-Jewish policy from legal persecution to
organised violence and presaging the
<a href="Holocaust" class="wikilink" title="Holocaust">Holocaust</a>.
The first organised <a href="Kindertransport" class="wikilink"
title="Kindertransport">Kindertransport</a> of Jewish children from
Germany and Austria to Britain began in December.

### 1939

The most commonly proposed date for the start of the war is September
1939, with the joint invasion of Poland by Nazi Germany and the USSR and
the subsequent declaration of war by the British and French empires
against Germany — though notably not against the USSR. The British and
French failure to launch any serious offensive against Germany during
the autumn and winter became known as the Phony War, and had disastrous
consequences for the small Allied initiative that remained.

The German–Soviet partnership had been formalised on 23 August in the
<a href="Molotov–Ribbentrop_Pact" class="wikilink"
title="Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact">Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact</a>, whose
secret protocols carved Eastern Europe into German and Soviet spheres.
(For full detail of the Soviet–Nazi relationship from the 1920s onwards,
see <a href="USSR_and_Nazi_Germany" class="wikilink"
title="USSR and Nazi Germany">USSR and Nazi Germany</a>.) Germany
invaded western Poland on 1 September; the USSR invaded eastern Poland
on 17 September; the two armies met along the agreed line and held a
joint victory parade at Brest-Litovsk. The Polish state was extinguished
by 6 October.

The USSR then pressured Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania into accepting
Soviet military bases on their territory, and on 30 November invaded
Finland in an attempt to enforce similar terms. The Finnish defenders,
despite enormous numerical disadvantage, fought the Red Army to a
standstill through the winter — exposing the catastrophic effects of
Stalin's
<a href="Great_Purge" class="wikilink" title="purges">purges</a> on the
Soviet officer corps and emboldening Hitler's later assessment of Soviet
military weakness.

### 1940

Britain and Germany both moved on Scandinavia in April. Germany invaded
Denmark and Norway, defeating the British expeditionary force and
securing iron-ore supplies from Sweden through Norwegian ports. The
Winter War ended in March with Finland forced to cede the Karelian
Isthmus to the USSR.

In May, Germany launched its western offensive, invading the
Netherlands, Belgium, Luxembourg and France in a six-week campaign that
destroyed the French army and drove the British Expeditionary Force back
across the Channel at <a href="Dunkirk_evacuation" class="wikilink"
title="Dunkirk">Dunkirk</a>. France was divided between German
occupation and the collaborationist
<a href="Vichy_France" class="wikilink" title="Vichy regime">Vichy
regime</a>. The USSR took advantage of Western collapse to annex
Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania and the Romanian region of
<a href="Bessarabia" class="wikilink" title="Bessarabia">Bessarabia</a>
outright. The United Kingdom occupied Iceland to prevent its falling
under Axis control.

The <a href="Battle_of_Britain" class="wikilink"
title="Battle of Britain">Battle of Britain</a> from July to October
ended the prospect of a German invasion across the Channel: the
<a href="Royal_Air_Force" class="wikilink" title="Royal Air Force">Royal
Air Force</a> denied the
<a href="Luftwaffe" class="wikilink" title="Luftwaffe">Luftwaffe</a> air
superiority and Hitler indefinitely postponed
<a href="Operation_Sea_Lion" class="wikilink"
title="Operation Sea Lion">Operation Sea Lion</a>. Italy entered the war
in June and launched its disastrous invasion of Greece in October.

In Eastern Europe,
<a href="anarchist_resistance_to_Nazi_Germany" class="wikilink"
title="anarchist resistance">anarchist resistance</a> networks began to
take shape — Italian, German, French, Polish and Spanish anarchists
already in exile or underground formed cells whose activities ranged
from intelligence and sabotage to direct armed action against the
occupiers.

### 1941

Italy was driven out of Ethiopia and Somalia by British and Commonwealth
forces, and its position in Greece collapsed. Germany invaded the
Balkans in April, occupying Yugoslavia and Greece in a matter of weeks,
and forming alliances with Hungary, Romania and Bulgaria. Conflict in
North Africa intensified between Italian-held Libya and British-held
Egypt, with the German
<a href="Afrika_Korps" class="wikilink" title="Afrika Korps">Afrika
Korps</a> dispatched under
<a href="Erwin_Rommel" class="wikilink" title="Erwin Rommel">Erwin
Rommel</a> to support Mussolini.

The German Blitz on British cities continued through the spring; the
<a href="Royal_Air_Force" class="wikilink" title="RAF">RAF</a> and
<a href="Royal_Navy" class="wikilink" title="Royal Navy">Royal Navy</a>
continued the Battle of the Atlantic against German U-boats — a campaign
that would prove decisive in keeping Britain in the war.

On 22 June, Germany launched
<a href="Operation_Barbarossa" class="wikilink"
title="Operation Barbarossa">Operation Barbarossa</a>, the largest land
invasion in history, against the USSR. The Soviet Union, having spent
nearly two years supplying the German war machine with oil, grain, and
ores, was caught catastrophically unprepared. Stalin had ignored
detailed warnings from British intelligence, his own spies, and multiple
defectors. Within six months Soviet casualties exceeded four million and
three million Soviet prisoners had been taken, of whom two million would
die in German captivity by February 1942. The disaster was directly
attributable to the prior decade of Stalinist policy: the
<a href="Molotov–Ribbentrop_Pact" class="wikilink"
title="Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact">Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact</a>, the
<a href="Great_Purge" class="wikilink"
title="purges of the officer corps">purges of the officer corps</a>, and
the offensive deployment of forward units. (See
<a href="USSR_and_Nazi_Germany" class="wikilink"
title="USSR and Nazi Germany">USSR and Nazi Germany</a> for the longer
history.)

In Yugoslavia, the <a href="Yugoslav_Partisans" class="wikilink"
title="Yugoslav Partisans">Yugoslav Partisans</a> under Tito and the
<a href="Chetniks" class="wikilink" title="Chetniks">Chetniks</a> under
Mihailović began the resistance war that would tie down major Axis
forces for the rest of the conflict. Across occupied Europe, anarchist,
communist, and broadly democratic resistance networks began to coalesce.

On 7 December, Japan attacked the United States naval base at
<a href="Pearl_Harbor" class="wikilink" title="Pearl Harbor">Pearl
Harbor</a> in Hawaii. Germany declared war on the United States four
days later. The entry of the United States and the USSR into the war is
widely regarded as the strategic catastrophe of the Axis: it brought
into the conflict the two largest land army and the largest industrial
economy on earth. From late 1941 the eventual defeat of the Axis was a
matter of time, attrition, and cost.

### 1942

The high-water mark of Axis expansion. In the Pacific, Japan overran the
Philippines, Malaya, Singapore, the Dutch East Indies and Burma in the
first half of the year, before the
<a href="Battle_of_Midway" class="wikilink"
title="Battle of Midway">Battle of Midway</a> in June destroyed four
Japanese aircraft carriers and reversed the strategic balance. The
American campaign at <a href="Guadalcanal" class="wikilink"
title="Guadalcanal">Guadalcanal</a> from August onwards began the long
Pacific island-hopping advance.

In North Africa, Rommel's advance was halted at the
<a href="Second_Battle_of_El_Alamein" class="wikilink"
title="Second Battle of El Alamein">Second Battle of El Alamein</a> in
October and November by Commonwealth forces under
<a href="Bernard_Montgomery" class="wikilink"
title="Montgomery">Montgomery</a>. The Anglo-American
<a href="Operation_Torch" class="wikilink"
title="Operation Torch">Operation Torch</a> landings in November opened
a second front in French North Africa.

In the East, the Wehrmacht reached the Volga and the Caucasus during the
summer offensive. The <a href="Battle_of_Stalingrad" class="wikilink"
title="Battle of Stalingrad">Battle of Stalingrad</a> began in August
and consumed the German Sixth Army through the autumn and winter;
encirclement of the German forces in November precipitated a defeat from
which the Wehrmacht in the East would never recover.

The mass killing of European Jews moved into its industrial phase. The
<a href="Wannsee_Conference" class="wikilink"
title="Wannsee Conference">Wannsee Conference</a> in January 1942
coordinated the
<a href="Holocaust" class="wikilink" title="Holocaust">Holocaust</a>'s
logistics; <a href="Auschwitz_concentration_camp" class="wikilink"
title="Auschwitz-Birkenau">Auschwitz-Birkenau</a>,
<a href="Treblinka" class="wikilink" title="Treblinka">Treblinka</a>,
<a href="Sobibor" class="wikilink" title="Sobibor">Sobibor</a> and
<a href="Bełżec" class="wikilink" title="Bełżec">Bełżec</a> became the
principal sites of systematic mass murder.

### 1943

Stalingrad surrendered on 2 February. The Soviet victory at the
<a href="Battle_of_Kursk" class="wikilink"
title="Battle of Kursk">Battle of Kursk</a> in July, the largest tank
battle in history, ended the Wehrmacht's offensive capability in the
East. The Red Army began the long advance westward that would carry it
to Berlin.

The <a href="Warsaw_Ghetto_Uprising" class="wikilink"
title="Warsaw Ghetto Uprising">Warsaw Ghetto Uprising</a> in April and
May was the largest single act of Jewish armed resistance during the
Holocaust. Surrounded and outgunned, the fighters held out for nearly a
month against German forces. The uprising became one of the most
powerful symbols of resistance under fascist occupation.

In the Mediterranean, Allied forces took Tunisia in May, ending the
North African campaign, and invaded
<a href="Allied_invasion_of_Sicily" class="wikilink"
title="Sicily">Sicily</a> in July. Mussolini fell from power on 25 July;
Italy surrendered in September, though German forces immediately
occupied northern and central Italy and the Italian campaign continued
for the rest of the war. Resistance partisans, including significant
anarchist participation around Carrara and Pistoia, became a major
military and political force in the German-occupied north.

### 1944

On 6 June, <a href="D-Day" class="wikilink"
title="Allied forces landed in Normandy">Allied forces landed in
Normandy</a> in the largest seaborne invasion in history. By August,
Paris was liberated — partly by the
<a href="French_Resistance" class="wikilink"
title="French Resistance">French Resistance</a> uprising in the city
itself, in which Spanish anarchist exiles serving in
<a href="La_Nueve" class="wikilink" title="La Nueve">La Nueve</a> of the
Free French were the first armoured unit to enter the city. France was
substantially liberated by the end of the year. The Allies entered
Belgium and Luxembourg; the German
<a href="Battle_of_the_Bulge" class="wikilink"
title="Ardennes counter-offensive">Ardennes counter-offensive</a> in
December was the last major Wehrmacht offensive of the war and was
contained by January.

In the East, <a href="Operation_Bagration" class="wikilink"
title="Operation Bagration">Operation Bagration</a> in June and July
destroyed Army Group Centre and pushed the Red Army into Poland and the
Balkans. The <a href="Warsaw_Uprising" class="wikilink"
title="Warsaw Uprising">Warsaw Uprising</a> in August, launched by the
Polish
<a href="Home_Army" class="wikilink" title="Home Army">Home Army</a> as
Soviet forces approached the city, was met by Soviet inaction across the
Vistula and crushed by the Germans over two months — a deliberate Soviet
decision that allowed the destruction of the non-communist Polish
resistance and shaped the post-war Soviet domination of Poland.

The <a href="Auschwitz_Uprising" class="wikilink"
title="Auschwitz Uprising">Auschwitz Uprising</a> in October, in which
Jewish <a href="Sonderkommando" class="wikilink"
title="Sonderkommando">Sonderkommando</a> prisoners destroyed one of the
crematoria, was one of the few successful acts of armed resistance
inside an extermination camp.

In the Pacific, the <a href="Battle_of_Leyte_Gulf" class="wikilink"
title="Battle of Leyte Gulf">Battle of Leyte Gulf</a> in October was the
largest naval engagement in history and effectively destroyed the
Imperial Japanese Navy as a strategic force. American forces returned to
the Philippines.

### 1945

Soviet forces took Warsaw in January and reached the Oder by February.
Anglo-American forces crossed the Rhine in March. Germany lost effective
control of France and Poland and was crushed between two massive Allied
advances, with some of the most intense fighting of the war occurring as
the Reich collapsed. Hitler killed himself on 30 April as Soviet troops
fought through Berlin. Many senior Nazi scientists, officers and
officials escaped — to South America, the
<a href="Operation_Paperclip" class="wikilink"
title="United States">United States</a>, or the USSR — under programmes
that traded their wartime expertise for impunity.

Germany surrendered unconditionally on 8 May. The full extent of the
Holocaust became visible to the world as Allied troops liberated the
camps. In the Pacific, the United States dropped atomic bombs on
<a href="Hiroshima" class="wikilink" title="Hiroshima">Hiroshima</a> on
6 August and
<a href="Nagasaki" class="wikilink" title="Nagasaki">Nagasaki</a> on 9
August, killing approximately 200,000 people, the great majority
civilians. The USSR entered the war against Japan on 8 August. Japan
surrendered on 15 August, with the formal capitulation signed on 2
September.

The end of the war was also the beginning of new conflicts. Soviet
forces occupied Eastern Europe, where they would impose Stalinist
regimes over the next four years. The European empires returned to their
colonies in Asia and Africa to find independence movements that would
not be put down again. The Cold War was already taking shape in the
spring of 1945, before Berlin had even fallen.

## Effects

World War II reshaped the world more thoroughly than any conflict before
or since. The geopolitical balance of power, the world economy, the
dominant political philosophies, and the trajectory of technology were
all transformed.

### Decolonisation

*Main article: <a href="Decolonisation" class="wikilink"
title="Decolonisation">Decolonisation</a>*

The war crippled the British, French, Dutch and Belgian empires by
destroying their metropolitan economies and military prestige, and
inspired a <a href="Timeline_of_Anti-Colonialism" class="wikilink"
title="new wave of anti-colonial revolts">new wave of anti-colonial
revolts</a> across Asia and Africa. Independence movements in India,
Indonesia, Vietnam, Algeria, Ghana, Kenya and elsewhere drew on the
democratic and anti-fascist rhetoric the Allies had used during the war
and demanded its extension to the colonised. Many had served in the
Allied armies; many returned home unwilling to accept the prewar order.
The decolonisation that followed was uneven, often violent, and shaped
by the emerging Cold War, but it transformed the political map of the
world within a generation.

### Economic Boom

The decades following the war saw a sustained economic expansion across
Western Europe, North America and Japan, accompanied by major advances
in technology and a great increase in the availability of consumer
goods. The boom rested on Keynesian state intervention, capital
controls, strong organised labour, and the political compromise that
produced <a href="Social_Democracy" class="wikilink"
title="social democracy">social democracy</a> across most of Western
Europe. Anarchists have generally regarded this settlement as a real
material gain for working people that nonetheless came at the cost of
the absorption of labour movements into national-state institutions — a
deal whose terms began to come apart from the 1970s onward.

### Cold War and the New Empires

The war ended with two superpowers facing each other across a divided
Europe. The
<a href="Cold_War" class="wikilink" title="Cold War">Cold War</a> that
followed reshaped global politics for the next four decades, drove the
nuclear arms race, fuelled proxy wars across the decolonising world, and
provided ideological cover for both American imperial expansion and
Soviet domination of Eastern Europe. Anti-colonial and revolutionary
movements were repeatedly forced to choose between the two blocs, often
to their long-term cost.

### Atomic Weapons

The bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki opened the nuclear age. The
development and use of atomic weapons by the United States, followed by
the Soviet Union in 1949, created the conditions for the threat of mass
annihilation that has shadowed every subsequent generation. Anarchists
were among the earliest and most consistent critics of nuclear weapons,
identifying them as the logical endpoint of state power's claim on the
lives of its citizens.

### The United Nations and the State System

The post-war settlement entrenched the
<a href="State_(Polity)" class="wikilink" title="state system">state
system</a> more thoroughly than any previous arrangement, with the
<a href="United_Nations" class="wikilink" title="United Nations">United
Nations</a> elevating sovereign states as the only legitimate actors in
international politics and codifying the principle of non-interference
between them. For anarchists this represented the formal closure of the
revolutionary possibilities the war had briefly opened, locking working
people into national containers and bestowing legitimacy on the very
institutions that had produced the catastrophe.

## Crimes Against Humanity

### Axis

The crimes of the Axis powers were vast in scale and explicit in their
genocidal intent. The
<a href="Holocaust" class="wikilink" title="Holocaust">Holocaust</a> saw
the systematic murder of approximately six million European Jews,
alongside the killing of Roma, disabled people, gay men, political
prisoners, Soviet prisoners of war, and others — a coordinated programme
of industrial extermination unprecedented in human history. The German
occupation of Eastern Europe was conducted on principles of racial
hierarchy that envisaged the deliberate starvation of tens of millions
of Slavs and the slave-labour exploitation of survivors.

Japanese forces conducted comparable atrocities across Asia. The
<a href="Nanjing_Massacre" class="wikilink"
title="Nanjing Massacre">Nanjing Massacre</a> killed several hundred
thousand Chinese civilians; the biological-warfare programmes of
<a href="Unit_731" class="wikilink" title="Unit 731">Unit 731</a>
subjected thousands of prisoners to live experimentation; the
<a href="comfort_women" class="wikilink"
title="&quot;comfort women&quot; system">"comfort women" system</a>
enslaved an estimated 200,000 women, predominantly Korean and Chinese,
in military brothels; the <a href="Bataan_Death_March" class="wikilink"
title="Bataan Death March">Bataan Death March</a> and the treatment of
Allied prisoners of war killed tens of thousands more.

Italian fascist forces conducted chemical-weapons attacks and reprisal
massacres in Ethiopia, Libya, Yugoslavia and Greece.

### Allied

Acknowledging the moral asymmetry of the war does not require silence
about Allied crimes. The deliberate area bombing of German cities by the
<a href="Royal_Air_Force" class="wikilink" title="Royal Air Force">Royal
Air Force</a> and
<a href="United_States_Army_Air_Forces" class="wikilink"
title="USAAF">USAAF</a> killed an estimated 400,000 to 600,000
civilians; the firebombing of
<a href="Bombing_of_Tokyo" class="wikilink" title="Tokyo">Tokyo</a> in
March 1945 killed approximately 100,000 people in a single night. The
atomic bombings of
<a href="Hiroshima" class="wikilink" title="Hiroshima">Hiroshima</a> and
<a href="Nagasaki" class="wikilink" title="Nagasaki">Nagasaki</a> killed
approximately 200,000 people, the great majority civilians, and exposed
many more to long-term radiation injury.

The Soviet Union conducted mass deportations of entire ethnic groups
during the war — Volga Germans, Crimean Tatars, Chechens, Ingush,
Kalmyks and others — under conditions that killed substantial fractions
of the deported populations. Soviet forces conducted mass rape on a vast
scale during the advance into Germany and Eastern Europe. The deliberate
inaction of the Red Army during the
<a href="Warsaw_Uprising" class="wikilink"
title="Warsaw Uprising">Warsaw Uprising</a> handed Polish anti-Nazi
resistance over to German destruction.

The Western Allies, while fighting fascism abroad, maintained colonial
rule over hundreds of millions in Asia and Africa, and continued legal
racial segregation in the United States and the Union of South Africa.
The wartime <a href="internment_of_Japanese_Americans" class="wikilink"
title="internment of Japanese Americans">internment of Japanese
Americans</a> and Japanese Canadians imprisoned over 100,000 people
without trial.

The point of cataloguing these is not to draw an equivalence between the
Allies and the Axis. The Axis programmes were genocidal in intent and
pursued as policy; the Allied crimes were neither universal nor
systematic in the same sense. The point is that the war was won by
states whose own conduct cannot underwrite a heroic mythology, and that
the post-war order built on that victory inherited the contradictions of
its makers.

## Resistance

### Anarchist Resistance

*Main article:
<a href="Anarchist_Resistance_To_Nazi_Germany" class="wikilink"
title="Anarchist Resistance To Nazi Germany">Anarchist Resistance To
Nazi Germany</a>*

Anarchists fought in resistance networks across occupied Europe and
beyond. Italian anarchists were prominent in the partisan war in the
north; French anarchists, including many Spanish exiles, fought in the
<a href="Maquis" class="wikilink" title="Maquis">Maquis</a>; the
<a href="La_Nueve" class="wikilink" title="La Nueve">La Nueve</a>
company of Spanish anarchists in
<a href="Free_French_Forces" class="wikilink" title="Free French">Free
French</a> service was the first armoured unit to enter liberated Paris.
German anarchists in exile and underground produced clandestine
literature and supported escape networks. Bulgarian anarchists conducted
partisan operations in the Balkans.

### Other Resistance

Communist, socialist, liberal-democratic and nationalist resistance
movements operated across occupied Europe, with significant variation in
scale, effectiveness, and political character. The
<a href="Yugoslav_Partisans" class="wikilink"
title="Yugoslav Partisans">Yugoslav Partisans</a>,
<a href="French_Resistance" class="wikilink"
title="French Resistance">French Resistance</a>,
<a href="Polish_Home_Army" class="wikilink"
title="Polish Home Army">Polish Home Army</a>,
<a href="Greek_People&#39;s_Liberation_Army" class="wikilink"
title="Greek People&#39;s Liberation Army">Greek People's Liberation
Army</a>, and the various Soviet partisan formations all played
substantial military and political roles. Resistance to Japanese
occupation was conducted across China, Korea, the Philippines, Burma,
Indochina and Indonesia.
