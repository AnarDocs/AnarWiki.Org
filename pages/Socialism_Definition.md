# Socialism Definitions

## Overview

<a href="Socialism" class="wikilink" title="Socialism">Socialism</a> is
a political and economic framework based on the social ownership of the
means of production, the abolition of
<a href="capitalist" class="wikilink" title="capitalist">capitalist</a>
exploitation, and the organization of production for human need rather
than private profit. A socialist is one who believes that the means of
production — land, factories, tools, infrastructure — ought to be held
in common rather than concentrated in private hands.

Socialism is the broader tradition from which
<a href="anarchism" class="wikilink" title="anarchism">anarchism</a>
emerges. Anarchists are socialists in that they reject
<a href="private_property" class="wikilink"
title="private property">private property</a> in the means of
production; they are distinguished from authoritarian socialists by
their insistence that any genuinely socialist society must also be
stateless, hierarchy-free, and organized from below.

## Dictionary and Encyclopedia Definitions

Classical dictionary and encyclopedia definitions consistently center
socialism on collective ownership and the subordination of production to
social need rather than profit:

*Collins Dictionary* — "Socialism is a set of political and economic
theories based on the belief that the means of production, distribution,
and exchange should be owned or regulated by the community as a
whole."[^1]

*Oxford Learners Dictionary* — "a set of political and economic theories
based on the belief that everyone has an equal right to a share of a
country's wealth and that the government should own and control the main
industries."[^2]

*Wikipedia, Socialism* — "Socialism is an economic and political system
based on public or collective ownership of the means of production. It
encompasses diverse schools of thought, all of which share the view that
capitalism is marked by social injustices that need to be resolved
through social ownership."[^3]

*Encyclopedia Britannica* — "Socialism, social and economic doctrine
that calls for public rather than private ownership or regulation of
property and natural resources. According to the socialist view,
individuals do not live or work in isolation but live in cooperation
with one another. Furthermore, everything that people produce is in some
sense a social product, and everyone who contributes to the production
of a good is entitled to a share in it. Society as a whole, therefore,
should own or at least control property for the benefit of all its
members."[^4]

*Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* — "Socialism refers to a broad set
of political and economic doctrines whose central claims are: (a) that
markets and private property in productive assets are, for various
reasons, inefficient, unjust, or both; and (b) that collective ownership
and democratic planning of productive assets would constitute an
improvement."[^5]

Across these definitions, certain core features recur:

- Social or collective ownership of the means of production
- Production organized for social need rather than private profit
- Opposition to capitalist exploitation and the wage system
- Some form of collective economic organisation

What the standard definitions largely leave open is *how* collective
ownership is organized — whether through a federation of workers,
autonomous communes, or some other structure. This is precisely where
anarchists part ways with authoritarian and state socialist traditions.

## The Concept of "Social"

The word "socialism" derives from the Latin *socius* — companion,
associate, ally — and the idea of the *social*: that which belongs to,
and is organized by, human beings in their relations with one another
rather than by any individual owner or ruler.

### Etymology

**Latin roots:**

- From Latin *socius* = "companion, ally, associate, partner"
- Related to *sequi* = "to follow" — originally suggesting those who
  travel together
- *Socialis* = "of companionship, of allies, living with others"
- *Societas* = "fellowship, association, union, community"

**Development of "social":**

- Latin *socialis* → Old French *social* → English *social* (16th
  century)
- Core meaning: pertaining to living together with others; communal; not
  individual or private

**Emergence of "socialism":**

- The term *socialism* appears in French and English sources around
  1827–1833
- First recorded use in French: *socialisme*, in the journal *Le Globe*,
  associated with the followers of
  <a href="Henri_de_Saint-Simon" class="wikilink"
  title="Henri de Saint-Simon">Henri de Saint-Simon</a>
- Early English use: in the *Co-operative Magazine* (1827), associated
  with <a href="Robert_Owen" class="wikilink" title="Robert Owen">Robert
  Owen</a>'s cooperative communities
- Used to contrast with *individualism* — socialism as the doctrine that
  human welfare is a collective rather than merely individual concern

### Structural Implications

**Collective over individual ownership:**

The root meaning — *socius*, companion — implies that productive
resources belong to those who share life together rather than to
isolated individuals. Socialism carries the implication that the earth
and its products are held in common by those who work with them and live
alongside one another.

**Production for the community:**

If production is *social* — carried out by people working together —
then its fruits are social too. Socialism follows the logic that
cooperative labour produces social wealth, and social wealth ought to
return to the community rather than accumulating in private hands.

**Solidarity and mutual obligation:**

*Socius* also carries the sense of ally and partner in a shared
endeavour. Socialism implies
<a href="mutual_aid" class="wikilink" title="mutual aid">mutual aid</a>
— an active obligation to one another — not merely passive coexistence.
This is why anarchists like
<a href="Peter_Kropotkin" class="wikilink" title="Peter Kropotkin">Peter
Kropotkin</a> grounded their socialism in the biological and historical
evidence for cooperation as the engine of human survival and
development.

**The question of the state:**

The word "social" does not inherently imply state management. A society
can organize its common affairs through voluntary association, federated
communes, workers' councils, or any number of non-state structures. It
is only the authoritarian variant of socialism that conflates social
ownership with state ownership. Anarchists insist the two are
incompatible: a state managing the economy on behalf of "society" is
still a ruling class managing the economy on its own behalf.

## What Socialism Is Not

A number of common misconceptions conflate socialism with things it is
not:

**Socialism is not the same as government spending.** A state that funds
public services within a capitalist economy is not socialist — it is a
regulated capitalism. The
<a href="NHS" class="wikilink" title="NHS">NHS</a>, public housing, or
state education are social reforms within capitalism, not socialism.

**Socialism is not the same as
<a href="state_capitalism" class="wikilink"
title="state capitalism">state capitalism</a>.** When a state owns
enterprises, employs workers for wages, and accumulates capital in the
manner of a private firm, this is state capitalism rather than
socialism. The <a href="USSR" class="wikilink" title="USSR">USSR</a> is
the paradigm case: workers did not own or control the means of
production; the party-state did.

**Socialism is not authoritarianism by definition.** The association of
socialism with one-party rule, gulags, and secret police is a product of
a particular historical moment, not a structural feature of socialist
thought. The libertarian socialist tradition long predates Bolshevism
and consistently opposed it.

**Socialism does not require poverty or equality of outcome.** Socialism
aims to organize production to meet everyone's needs, not to level
everyone to minimum subsistence. The
<a href="anarchist_communist" class="wikilink"
title="anarchist communist">anarchist communist</a> slogan "from each
according to ability, to each according to need" implies abundance
organized equitably, not uniform austerity.

## Quotes About Socialism

**<a href="Mikhail_Bakunin" class="wikilink"
title="Mikhail Bakunin">Mikhail Bakunin</a>** — "We are convinced that
freedom without Socialism is privilege and injustice, and that Socialism
without freedom is slavery and brutality."[^6]

**<a href="Peter_Kropotkin" class="wikilink" title="Peter Kropotkin">Peter
Kropotkin</a>** — "Anarchism is a synthesis of the two great movements
of thought in the economical and the political fields: Socialism and
Liberalism."[^7]

**<a href="Errico_Malatesta" class="wikilink"
title="Errico Malatesta">Errico Malatesta</a>** — "We anarchists do not
want to emancipate the people; we want the people to emancipate
themselves."[^8]

**<a href="Oscar_Wilde" class="wikilink" title="Oscar Wilde">Oscar
Wilde</a>** — "It is to be noted that those who have opposed Socialism,
and are opposed to it still, are those who have been most successful
under the present system. The others are indifferent."[^9]
