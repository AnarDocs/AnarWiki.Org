**Anarchism in Spain** refers to the presence of
<a href="anarchism" class="wikilink" title="anarchism">anarchism</a> in
the country of <a href="Spain" class="wikilink" title="Spain">Spain</a>.

First emerging in the 1840s among early followers of
<a href="Pierre-Joseph_Proudhon" class="wikilink"
title="Proudhon">Proudhon</a>, it soon spread after a visit by
<a href="Giuseppe_Fanelli" class="wikilink"
title="Giuseppe Fanelli">Giuseppe Fanelli</a> in 1868. Anarchism was a
popular and influential idea, playing a major role in the politics and
history of the late 1800s and early 1900s in Spain. The apex of
anarchism was reached in the 1930s, where anarchists attempted several
insurrections and were a major faction in the
<a href="Spanish_Civil_War" class="wikilink"
title="Spanish Civil War">Spanish Civil War</a>. Anarchists lost the war
and were forced underground by the new dictatorship, often fighting in
guerilla groups known as "Maquis". With the end of the Spanish
dictatorship in 1975, anarchist organizations were legalised and have
continued to exist in Spain. Spain has been noted for having a strong
squatters movement.
