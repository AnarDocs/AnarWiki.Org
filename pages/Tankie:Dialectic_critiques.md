# Critiques Of Marxist-Leninist Dialectics

## Summary

Us humans are pattern recognising animals - we like looking for
patterns, finding them, arranging them, and interpreting the world
through them. This can be harmless, informative, and in the case of pure
science even transformative.

Dialectic materialism is an abstract and somewhat ambiguous metaphorical
analytical model / framework for analysing something after the fact. It
can be useful for identifying contradictions and tensions, but when it
goes beyond its limitations it can also mystify more then clarify.

There are non-Leninists who use Dialectical Materialism and even some
anarchists (libertarian Marxism, Council communists etc.) who do too.
Bakunin initially embraced it, but later rejected it. But at best it is
only scientific in the sense that social sciences are scientific if
that, and some would argue that because it is unfalsifiable that it
doesn't even qualify as a science in that sense either.

Marxists, including Marx, have used it to predict all sorts of things
that didn't end up happening (such as (The imminent collapse of
capitalism under its own contradictions), but selectively choose aspects
of the past as a proof of its reliability. It has also been accused of
often being (in some of its uses) eurocentric, hierarchal,
authoritarian, and deterministic in its assumptions.

## Tankie Use Of Dialectics

Dialectics are used by Tankies to justify anything, claim everything,
ignore the need to deal with any inconvenient facts, and accuse anyone
who disagrees with them as not understanding it well enough. It is
useful for them as a 'get out of jail card' for them to avoid having to
face the logical contradictions and consequences of their positions. For
these reasons, non-Marxist anarchists have compared their use of
dialectics to scriptural interpretation.

## Anti-Dialectical Dialogues

### The laws of dialectics

*Q:* How do we distinguish between a genuine dialectical contradiction
and just… two things being in tension?

*M:* A dialectical contradiction is one where the opposing forces drive
each other's development.

*Q:* And how do we identify which tensions are "dialectical" in that
sense?

*M:* By analysing them through the dialectical method.

**Circular.** The method identifies dialectical contradictions, and
dialectical contradictions validate the method.

### The base and superstructure

*Q:* Historical materialism says the economic base determines the
cultural and political superstructure. Does that mean economic
conditions cause everything in society?

*M:* Not mechanically — the superstructure can react back on the base.
It's a dialectical relationship.

*Q:* So the base determines the superstructure, except when the
superstructure shapes the base?

*M:* Yes — they're in a dialectical unity.

**Dead end.** This is true of almost all social phenomena. What would it
mean for this claim to be wrong?

### Historical inevitability

*Q:* Historical materialism says capitalism will inevitably be
superseded by socialism. What would count as evidence that this
prediction was wrong?

*M:* The contradictions of capitalism are still playing out — the
timeline is long. We see the evidence in every crisis.

*Q:* But capitalism has survived many crises. At what point would you
update the theory?

*M:* Capitalism surviving crises is itself a sign of its increasing
instability — it has to work harder to sustain itself.

**Retrofitted.** Confirmation and disconfirmation are treated as equally
supportive. This is not a prediction — it's an unfalsifiable narrative.

### The vanguard party and false consciousness

*Q:* If the working class doesn't support the revolution, Leninists say
they suffer from "false consciousness." What would genuine working-class
consciousness look like, and how do we tell the difference?

*M:* True class consciousness means understanding one's objective
interests — that capitalism is exploitative and must be overthrown.

*Q:* Who decides what the objective interests of the working class are?

*M:* The vanguard party, guided by scientific Marxism.

**Circular.** Workers who agree with the party have class consciousness.
Workers who disagree have false consciousness. The party is never wrong
— only the workers are.

### The withering of the state

*Q:* Marx and Engels said the state would wither away after the
revolution. In every actually existing socialist state it expanded
massively. How does the theory account for that?

*M:* Because of capitalist encirclement — hostile external pressure
forces the socialist state to strengthen itself defensively.

*Q:* So the state can never wither away as long as capitalism exists
anywhere in the world?

*M:* Essentially, yes — it requires global conditions.

*Q:* Then the prediction is untestable within any realistic timeframe or
circumstance. Isn't that equivalent to saying the theory can't be
falsified?

*M:* We have to be patient — history moves dialectically.

**Retrofitted.** The withering-state prediction has been retroactively
condition-gated to an impossibly remote future. Like a cheque that can
only be cashed when conditions that will never obtain finally arrive.

### The theory of stages

*Q:* Historical materialism holds that all societies must pass through
fixed stages — primitive communism, slavery, feudalism, capitalism,
socialism. Is that a universal law?

*M:* Yes — it reflects the material development of productive forces.
Each stage creates the conditions for the next.

*Q:* But many societies didn't follow this sequence. Pre-colonial
Africa, the Americas, large parts of Asia — they had no period of
classical slavery in the Greco-Roman sense. Do they not count?

*M:* The stages can overlap or compress — some societies skip stages or
pass through them rapidly under external pressure.

**Retrofitted.** A universal law that admits unlimited exceptions and
compressions is not a law — it's a loose narrative that can be fitted to
any history after the fact.

## Studies Critical of Marxist-Leninist Dialectics

Criticisms of Dialectics are usually of these types:

- Lack of Empirical Validation: Critics argue that dialectical laws
  (like the negation of the negation) are not scientific but rather
  quasi-religious or metaphysical.
- Determinism: The fear that if history is guided by internal
  dialectical contradictions, human agency is reduced to merely playing
  a predestined role.
- Misapplication to Nature: Many critics (including some Marxists) argue
  that applying dialectics to the natural sciences (as Engels did) is a
  category error.
- Illogical leaps: Leninist claim is made - Dialectical Materialist
  analysis applied and supports claim - claim becomes unquestionable on
  its own merits.
