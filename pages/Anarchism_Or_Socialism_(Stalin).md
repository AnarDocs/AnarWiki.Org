# Anarchism AND socialism: a response to Stalin's false dichotomy

## Introduction: the manufactured opposition

In 1906-07, Joseph Stalin penned 'Anarchism or Socialism?' — a work that
became a template for authoritarian misrepresentation of anarchist
thought. Writing under the pressure of revolutionary upheaval, Stalin
sought to discredit anarchism not through honest engagement but by
constructing elaborate straw figures to demolish. His opening gambit
reveals the entire game:

> 'The cornerstone of anarchism is the individual, whose emancipation,
> according to its tenets, is the principal condition for the
> emancipation of the masses… The cornerstone of Marxism, however, is
> the masses, whose emancipation, according to its tenets, is the
> principal condition for the emancipation of the individual.'

This manufactured opposition between individual and collective
liberation serves a specific purpose: positioning state socialism as the
only 'scientific' path whilst dismissing anarchism as petty-bourgeois
idealism. The framing fundamentally misunderstands, or deliberately
misrepresents, anarchist philosophy.

## Part I: the dialectical muddle

### The false dichotomy of individual vs collective

Stalin claims that anarchists proclaim 'Everything for the individual'
whilst Marxists champion 'Everything for the masses.' He insists:
'Clearly, we have here two principles, one negating the other, and not
merely disagreements on tactics.'

This crude binary thinking reflects either profound ignorance or
calculated deception. Anarcho-communists do not treat individual and
collective liberation as opposing forces requiring one to be
subordinated to the other. The freedom of each person is not limited by
the freedom of all — it is made possible by it. When Kropotkin wrote
about mutual aid, he was not advocating isolated individualism but
describing how cooperation and solidarity are fundamental to both
personal and collective welfare. Anarchism rejects both the capitalist
myth of the isolated individual and the authoritarian subjugation of
persons to an abstract 'collective' managed by party bureaucrats.

### Dialectics without dogma

Stalin dedicates considerable space to defending dialectical
materialism, spending pages attacking anarchists for supposedly being
metaphysicians. He writes:

> 'Dialectics tells us that nothing in the world is eternal, everything
> in the world is transient and mutable… that is why dialectics regards
> everything critically; that is why it denies the existence of a
> once-and-for-all established truth.'

The irony is considerable. His own party would establish its
interpretation of Marxism as precisely such an eternal truth, with
dissent punished as counter-revolutionary heresy. Stalin accuses
anarchists of metaphysics whilst his dialectical method somehow always
arrives at the necessity of state power, party dictatorship, and the
suppression of dissent.

Stalin claims Proudhon was a metaphysician because he believed in
'immutable justice.' But Proudhon's critique of property — 'property is
theft' — was a material analysis of exploitation, not metaphysical
speculation. The anarchist tradition has continuously evolved through
practice and theory: from Proudhon through Bakunin, Kropotkin,
Malatesta, and Goldman, ideas were refined in response to changing
conditions rather than fixed in place by party decree.

### The question of revolutionary agency

Stalin presents revolution as something that occurs when 'productive
forces have sufficiently matured,' quoting Marx: 'No social order ever
perishes before all the productive forces for which there is room in it
have developed.'

This mechanical materialism treats revolution as an inevitable outcome
of economic development rather than the conscious action of people
seeking liberation. Stalin writes: 'Evolution prepares for revolution
and creates the ground for it; revolution consummates the process of
evolution and facilitates its further activity.'

The historical record sits awkwardly with this schema. The Paris Commune
did not wait for productive forces to mature. The Ukrainian Free
Territory emerged not from economic inevitability but from peasants
deciding to take control of their own lives. Revolutionary Spain saw
workers and peasants reorganising society immediately, without
consulting historical schemas about the correct stage of development.
Stalin's determinism served a clear function: if revolution follows
scientific laws, the party that understands those laws must guide the
masses, making the vanguard necessary not by workers' choice but by
historical decree.

### The slander of scientific method

Throughout this section, Stalin repeatedly accuses anarchists of being
unscientific. He mocks their supposed inability to understand that 'the
same democratic republic can be 'good' and 'bad' at the same time.'

Anarchists understand perfectly well that phenomena can have
contradictory aspects. The objection is to using dialectical flexibility
to justify whatever the party decides. If the democratic republic is
both good and bad, party dictatorship can be justified by the same logic
— 'good' insofar as it suppresses the bourgeoisie, 'bad' only in some
abstract future sense that never arrives.

The anarchist approach to social transformation is empirical in
character. It draws on actual experiments in non-hierarchical
organisation, on successes and failures in workers' control, on
practical experience rather than genuflection before nineteenth-century
texts treated as repositories of eternal truth.

## Part II: the materialist mystification

### The crude reduction of anarchist thought

Stalin begins his discussion of materialism by attempting to paint
anarchists as confused idealists. He claims they don't understand that
'social being determines consciousness.' He presents his famous
shoemaker parable:

> 'This proletarianised shoemaker goes on working, but finds that it is
> a very difficult matter to save money… Here our shoemaker realises
> that in order to improve his conditions he must fight the masters and
> not open a workshop of his own.'

The story is revealing in what it omits: any mention of the worker's own
agency, creativity, or capacity for self-organisation. The shoemaker is
portrayed as a passive vessel whose consciousness mechanically reflects
his economic position. Workers do not simply absorb ideas based on their
conditions — they actively interpret experiences, discuss with comrades,
experiment with resistance, and develop understanding through struggle.

Stalin writes: 'It is not the consciousness of men that determines their
being, but, on the contrary, their social being that determines their
consciousness.' That material conditions shape consciousness is not in
dispute. What anarchists reject is the mechanical determinism
Marxist-Leninists use to justify their rule — the idea that workers
require an enlightened vanguard to interpret their conditions for them.

### The question of form and content

Stalin argues: 'content is impossible without form, but the point is
that since a given form lags behind its content, it never fully
corresponds to this content; and so the new content is 'obliged' to
clothe itself for a time in the old form, and this causes a conflict
between them.'

This abstract philosophising conceals a political agenda. Stalin uses
the form/content distinction to argue that economic development must
precede political transformation — conveniently justifying whatever
political form the party imposes as historically necessary. The
Bolshevik state did not wither away as economic conditions developed. It
became more entrenched, generating a new ruling class of bureaucrats
whose position depended on maintaining the very structures that were
supposedly transitional.

### Material conditions and human agency

The most damaging aspect of Stalin's materialism is how it erases human
agency. He states: 'we must seek the grounds for a given ideal not in
the minds of men, not in their imaginations, but in the development of
their economic conditions.'

This is not materialism — it is fatalism. Real materialism acknowledges
that whilst history is not made under conditions of one's choosing, it
is still made by people. Ideas become material forces when they take
hold of masses of people. The belief in self-organisation can transform
material reality, as revolutionary moments have shown repeatedly.

Stalin accuses anarchists of saying 'man is what he eats' whilst
simultaneously calling this vulgar materialism. He writes: 'Don't you
really know that physiological phenomena, such as eating, for example,
differ fundamentally from sociological phenomena?' The accusation is a
strawman. The anarchist argument is that authoritarian political forms
can become obstacles to economic liberation, that means shape ends, that
a free society cannot be built through dictatorship.

## Part III: the dictatorship deception

### The poisoned well of 'scientific' socialism

Stalin's third section reveals the authoritarian project most clearly.
He writes:

> 'The dialectical method affirms that only that class which is growing
> day by day, which always marches forward and fights unceasingly for a
> better future, can be progressive to the end.'

This sounds revolutionary, but it sets up the need for interpreters —
those who determine which class is 'growing' and what constitutes
'marching forward.' The party positions itself as the scientific
authority determining history's direction and, by extension, everyone
else's obligations within it.

### The Paris Commune: misreading revolution

Stalin extensively quotes Engels: 'Do you want to know what this
dictatorship looks like? Look at the Paris Commune. That was the
Dictatorship of the Proletariat.'

Stalin fundamentally misreads what made the Commune revolutionary. He
focuses on it being the 'dictatorship of the proletariat' whilst
ignoring its distinctly anarchist features: the immediate recall of all
delegates, workers' wages for all officials, direct democracy in place
of representative government, the armed people replacing standing
armies, and no separation between legislative and executive power.

Stalin quotes Arthur Arnould describing the Commune: 'This dictatorship
lasted uninterruptedly for over two months… the people were the rulers,
the only rulers, they themselves set up their police and magistracy.'
The people were the rulers — not a party claiming to represent them.
This is precisely what anarchism advocates, and precisely what the
Bolshevik party would destroy at Kronstadt and elsewhere.

### The state that won't wither

Stalin quotes Marx and Engels extensively about the state withering
away:

> 'The working class in the course of its development will substitute
> for the old bourgeois society an association which will exclude
> classes and their antagonism, and there will be no more political
> power properly so-called.'

And Engels:

> 'The state, then, has not existed from all eternity… We are now
> rapidly approaching a stage in the development of production at which
> the existence of these classes… will become a positive hindrance to
> production. They will fall as inevitably as they arose… Along with
> them the state will inevitably fall.'

Stalin's own practice would prove these predictions hollow. The
'workers' state' became a state over workers. The 'transitional period'
became permanent. The dictatorship of the proletariat became
dictatorship of the party, then of the central committee, then of Stalin
himself.

### Manufacturing anarchist positions

Stalin repeatedly attributes positions to anarchists that anarchism does
not hold. He claims anarchists believe in 'state capitalism' where 'each
must work as much as he likes, or as much as the state determines.' He
says anarchists want to preserve 'representative government and
wage-labour.'

The irony is sharp. Stalin's Soviet Union established precisely what he
accused anarchists of wanting: state capitalism with party bosses, wage
labour under 'socialist' management, and representative government
collapsing into party dictatorship. Anarchism advocates the abolition of
wage labour entirely, not its management by a 'workers' state.' It seeks
the free association of producers, not state-directed production. Stalin
attributed to anarchism precisely what his own system would create.

### The violence question

Stalin accuses anarchists of rejecting revolutionary violence, quoting
supposed anarchist texts claiming Marxists believe 'only by means of
ballot papers… can parties capture power.'

The accusation is difficult to sustain. Anarchists have consistently
engaged in revolutionary struggle — the Black Army in Ukraine fought
both Whites and Reds, Spanish anarchists fought fascism with arms, and
the propaganda of the deed was whatever else it was but not pacifist.
What anarchism rejects is the institutionalisation of violence in a
permanent state apparatus. Stalin quotes Marx approvingly: 'The
proletariat will use its political supremacy to wrest, by degrees, all
capital from the bourgeoisie.' But 'political supremacy' exercised
through state power creates new forms of domination. Authoritarian
methods produce authoritarian outcomes.

## Beyond the state socialist betrayal

### Every 'workers' state' becomes a state over workers

Stalin proclaims: 'The dictatorship of the proletariat will be a
dictatorship of the entire proletariat as a class over the bourgeoisie
and not the domination of a few individuals over the proletariat.'

History has not been kind to this claim. Every 'dictatorship of the
proletariat' has become a dictatorship over the proletariat. The Soviet
Union crushed the Kronstadt sailors' call for 'Soviets without
Bolsheviks.' The Bolsheviks dismantled the factory committees that had
practised workers' control. Independent unions were abolished in favour
of party-controlled organisations. Workers who struck were shot as
counter-revolutionaries. Stalin perfected this system, but its
foundations were laid by the very conception of revolutionary party
dictatorship he was advocating.

### The question of scale

Stalin argues that socialism must be built on 'the broad basis of modern
large-scale industry' and mocks what he calls Kropotkin's 'scattered'
community socialism. He insists: 'we must welcome the expansion of
production and regard as the basis of future socialism not separate
towns and communities, but the entire and indivisible territory of the
whole state.'

This conflates two distinct questions: the scale of production and the
scale of political control. Large-scale industry need not imply
hierarchical management. Factories can be run by workers' councils;
railways can be coordinated through federal agreements. Stalin's
insistence on centralisation serves political purposes — by declaring
that socialism requires state-wide coordination, it justifies party
control over all aspects of life. The Spanish collectives demonstrated
that federal coordination could manage complex economies without a
central state.

## The synthesis: anarchist communism as true socialism

### Who are the genuine socialists?

Stalin concludes his work claiming anarchists 'are not genuine
Socialists' and 'not genuine revolutionaries.' Examining who actually
upholds socialist principles requires looking at the record rather than
the rhetoric.

Stalin's system created a new class division between party bureaucrats
and workers; anarchism seeks to abolish hierarchy entirely. Stalin
advocated party control over workers; anarchist communism demands direct
workers' control through their own organisations. Stalin's state never
withered away; anarchists work to make it irrelevant by building
alternative structures from below. Stalin maintained wage labour and
inequality; the Spanish anarchist collectives implemented needs-based
distribution from the outset.

### The living revolutionary tradition

Wherever working people take direct control, they tend toward anarchist
practices regardless of the theoretical frameworks on offer. The Paris
Commune that Stalin himself invokes practised direct democracy and the
recall of delegates. Russian workers created factory committees
practising self-management before the Bolsheviks dismantled them.
Spanish workers and peasants organised production without bosses or
states. Hungarian workers' councils in 1956 rejected both capitalism and
'socialist' party rule. The Zapatistas have practised autonomous
self-governance without capturing state power.

The revolutionary impulse points toward horizontal organisation. It is
'revolutionary' parties that impose hierarchy onto movements that, left
to themselves, develop along egalitarian lines.

### Means and ends

Stalin writes: 'strikes, boycott, parliamentarism, meetings and
demonstrations are all good forms of struggle as means for preparing and
organising the proletariat. But not one of these means is capable of
abolishing existing inequality.'

He is right that these alone are insufficient. But party dictatorship
does not abolish inequality either — it creates new forms of it, between
party and masses, between political directors and the workers they
direct. Hierarchical revolutionary organisations produce hierarchical
post-revolutionary societies. A free society cannot be built through
dictatorship, for the same reason that the means of struggle shape the
character of what is built.

## Conclusion: anarchism AND socialism

'Anarchism or Socialism?' Stalin's question assumes a forced choice
between freedom and equality, between liberty and solidarity. Anarchism
without socialism tends toward individualist chaos or, worse,
'anarcho'-capitalism — a contradiction in which private property simply
recreates hierarchy under a different name. Socialism without anarchism
produces state capitalism, where party bureaucrats become the new ruling
class.

Anarchist socialism — communist anarchism — offers a different path:
free individuals cooperating as equals, with common affairs managed
through voluntary federal agreements rather than state commands. Stalin
wanted to force this false choice because his 'socialism' required
submission to party dictatorship. But the destination of that road is
now part of the historical record. Every 'workers' state' has betrayed
workers. Every 'transitional period' has become permanent. Every party
claiming to represent the proletariat has ended as a force above it.

The revolution is not a future event to be managed by specialists
interpreting historical laws. It is the ongoing process of replacing
domination with cooperation, replacing exploitation with mutual aid.
Strikes that bypass bureaucratic channels, factories occupied and run
collectively, communities building mutual support networks — these are
moments where anarchist socialism emerges from below rather than being
handed down from above.

Stalin's great fear, concealed beneath mockery and misrepresentation,
was that workers might conclude they have no need of shepherds. His
entire theoretical edifice — the dialectical materialism that always
concludes in party rule, the historical stages that always require
vanguard guidance, the dictatorship that never withers — exists to
convince workers otherwise.

The most instructive revolutionary moments have been anarchist in
practice, whatever their participants called themselves. When workers
take control, the tendency is not to recreate old hierarchies with new
personnel but to build new forms of organisation based on equality and
mutual accountability. The choice is between genuine liberation and new
forms of domination wrapped in revolutionary rhetoric.
