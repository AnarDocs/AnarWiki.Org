# Utopian Socialism

## Overview

**Utopian socialism** is a term applied, mostly retrospectively and
mostly by
<a href="Marxism" class="wikilink" title="Marxists">Marxists</a>, to the
early-nineteenth-century socialist currents associated with
<a href="Henri_de_Saint-Simon" class="wikilink"
title="Henri de Saint-Simon">Henri de Saint-Simon</a>,
<a href="Charles_Fourier" class="wikilink"
title="Charles Fourier">Charles Fourier</a>,
<a href="Robert_Owen" class="wikilink" title="Robert Owen">Robert
Owen</a>, and
<a href="Étienne_Cabet" class="wikilink" title="Étienne Cabet">Étienne
Cabet</a>. The label was popularised by
<a href="Friedrich_Engels" class="wikilink"
title="Friedrich Engels">Friedrich Engels</a> in *Socialism: Utopian and
Scientific* (1880), where it served to distinguish these earlier
socialists — treated as well-meaning pioneers limited by the immaturity
of capitalism in their time — from the "scientific socialism" Engels and
<a href="Karl_Marx" class="wikilink" title="Karl Marx">Karl Marx</a>
claimed to be developing.[^1]

From an anarchist standpoint the category is useful but its usual
framing is not. The Marxist critique of the utopians is methodological:
they are said to have derived socialism from abstract reason and moral
appeal, to have addressed themselves to society at large — and by
preference to the propertied — and to have imagined socialism as a
blueprint to be demonstrated and adopted rather than as the outcome of
class antagonism. The charges have force against Owen's lobbying of
factory owners and aristocrats, or Fourier's reported habit of waiting
at home for the rich benefactor who would fund his phalansteries. But
Marxism's positive alternative — the capture of the state apparatus by a
vanguard, on the expectation that it will dissolve the class relations
it was built to enforce — turns out, on its own materialist terms, to be
the more idealist proposition. Anarchism takes the genuine critique of
the utopians without taking on the Marxist solution, and on several
points its own tradition stands closer to what Marx praised in the
<a href="Paris_Commune" class="wikilink" title="Paris Commune">Paris
Commune</a> than to what Engels dismissed in Owen.

## Origins

The utopian socialists wrote in the aftermath of the French Revolution
and at the opening of large-scale industrial capitalism. Saint-Simon
proposed a rationally administered industrial society directed by
scientists, engineers and industrialists. Fourier elaborated an
extraordinarily detailed scheme of phalansteries — co-operative
communities of around 1,600 people, organised by his classification of
human passions and personality types. Owen, a Welsh industrialist, ran
<a href="New_Lanark" class="wikilink" title="New Lanark">New Lanark</a>
in Scotland as a model factory community from 1800, and later attempted
communitarian settlements at
<a href="New_Harmony,_Indiana" class="wikilink"
title="New Harmony, Indiana">New Harmony, Indiana</a> and elsewhere.
Cabet's Icarian communities pursued a Christian-inflected communism in
France and the United States.

What unites these projects, beyond their nineteenth-century setting, is
a particular relation to social change. Each begins from a worked-out
design for a better society. Each then seeks adopters: through
patronage, through demonstration, through persuasion across class lines.
The propertied are not the enemy but the audience. The poor will
benefit, but they are not the agents of the transformation; the plan is.

A later wave of utopian writing extends some of these impulses into
fiction.
<a href="Edward_Bellamy" class="wikilink" title="Edward Bellamy">Edward
Bellamy</a>'s *<a href="Looking_Backward" class="wikilink"
title="Looking Backward">Looking Backward</a>* (1888) depicted a future
America organised as a single nationalised Industrial Army with
compulsory labour from twenty-one to forty.
<a href="William_Morris" class="wikilink" title="William Morris">William
Morris</a> wrote *<a href="News_from_Nowhere" class="wikilink"
title="News from Nowhere">News from Nowhere</a>* (1890) partly in revolt
against Bellamy's regimented vision, drawing on his own libertarian
socialism and on Fourier's concern with pleasurable, creative work.
Later still, <a href="Ursula_K._Le_Guin" class="wikilink"
title="Ursula K. Le Guin">Ursula K. Le Guin</a>'s
*<a href="The_Dispossessed" class="wikilink" title="The Dispossessed">The
Dispossessed</a>* (1974) imagined an anarchist society in considerable
sociological detail — without thereby presenting itself as a blueprint
to be implemented.

## Engels's critique and the anarchist response

Engels's complaint against the utopians was that they treated socialism
as a system to be invented and persuaded into existence rather than as
something arising from the contradictions of capitalist production
itself. They saw bad ideas and good ideas where they should have seen
class struggle; they appealed to "society at large" where they should
have organised the proletariat. The reformability of capitalists, on
this reading, was downstream of the deeper error of idealising and
schematising in place of materialist analysis.

The charge does not transfer to anarchism.
<a href="Peter_Kropotkin" class="wikilink" title="Peter Kropotkin">Peter
Kropotkin</a>'s argument in
*<a href="The_Conquest_of_Bread" class="wikilink"
title="The Conquest of Bread">The Conquest of Bread</a>* and
*<a href="Fields,_Factories_and_Workshops" class="wikilink"
title="Fields, Factories and Workshops">Fields, Factories and
Workshops</a>* is precisely an argument from material conditions: the
productive capacity to provision everyone already exists, and the
bottleneck is the property relation, not technological immaturity. That
is not voluntarism dressed up as a blueprint; it is a claim about where
humanity already stands materially. Where anarchists do disagree with
Marxists is over whether the state apparatus can be captured and turned
to revolutionary ends, or whether the apparatus itself generates the
class relations it is supposed to abolish. That is an empirical and
historical question — and one on which the twentieth century gave
anarchists rather a lot of material to point at — not a moral one.

Anarchism is therefore not utopian socialism in any of the senses Engels
meant. Utopian socialism derives its programme from abstract reason and
moral appeal, asking the existing order to recognise a better blueprint.
Anarchism derives its programme from analysis of existing material
conditions: the productive capacities already developed under
capitalism, the antagonisms between classes, and the concrete experience
of working people building counter-institutions in the present. Utopian
socialism locates the agent of change in enlightened benefactors and
rational persuasion across class lines. Anarchism locates it in the
self-organised activity of the exploited and dominated, who transform
conditions through their own struggle rather than waiting to be granted
a better arrangement. Utopian socialism imagines socialism as a system
to be designed and demonstrated alongside capitalism. Anarchism
understands the new society as something built through direct conflict
with capital and the state — prefigurative organising develops the
capacities a free society requires, but does so as part of an active
struggle, not as a model meant to win over the ruling class by example.

Put more sharply: utopian socialism asks power to reform itself in light
of better ideas; anarchism organises the dispossessed to dismantle power
through their own collective action.

## Tendencies

### The blueprint problem

The most enduring objection to the utopians, shared by anarchists and
Marxists alike, is the blueprint. A finished design for the future,
presented as the future, tends to produce a politics of adoption rather
than struggle, and to install in advance the authority of whoever drew
up the plan. <a href="Mikhail_Bakunin" class="wikilink"
title="Mikhail Bakunin">Mikhail Bakunin</a> put the point in terms of
conceptual contestability: socialists shared commitments to equality,
freedom, justice and human dignity, but the meaning of these ideas was
always open and took on a particular hue when it was "mapped by a few
sages or savants".[^2] The role of the anarchist was not to draw the map
but to clear the conditions in which ordinary people could negotiate
meanings for themselves.
<a href="Rudolf_Rocker" class="wikilink" title="Rudolf Rocker">Rudolf
Rocker</a> reiterated this on principle: anarchism, he wrote, "rejects
all absolute schemes and concepts" and recognises no "definite final
goals for human development".[^3]

### Imagination without blueprint

Having ideals is not the same as deriving one's politics from them. Marx
and Engels reserved "utopian" for socialists whose method was to
construct rational systems and seek their adoption through persuasion,
including persuasion of the propertied. A socialist who imagines a free
society in vivid detail but grounds their strategy in material
conditions, existing struggles and the self-activity of the oppressed is
doing something quite different — and arguably what Marx himself did
when he wrote about the Paris Commune, or when in *The German Ideology*
he pictured a communist society in which one might "hunt in the morning,
fish in the afternoon, rear cattle in the evening, criticise after
dinner ... without ever becoming hunter, fisherman, shepherd or critic".

Anarchists have written abundantly in this register. Kropotkin's
*Conquest of Bread* sketches the provisioning of a free city in concrete
terms; Le Guin's *Dispossessed* explores an anarchist economy and its
discontents at novel length; Morris's *News from Nowhere* walks the
reader through a post-revolutionary England of pleasurable work and
abolished riches. None of these is a blueprint in Engels's sense. None
addresses itself to the propertied. Each is a tool for imagining what is
already implicit in struggle.

### Prefiguration as the anarchist alternative

Where the utopian socialist offers a model, the anarchist offers
prefiguration: the principle that means must embody the ends they
pursue, so that the relations and capacities of a free society are built
up in the course of dismantling the old. Ruth Kinna describes the
relationship as triadic — utopian imagination mediates the means and
ends of action, "injecting it with a set of possibilities that make
sense of their ethical inter-relationship".[^4] Prefiguration is not a
recipe for the future but a discipline of the present: mutual aid,
<a href="federalism" class="wikilink"
title="federations">federations</a>, co-operatives, occupations,
<a href="autonomy" class="wikilink" title="autonomous">autonomous</a>
spaces, and the everyday refusals through which people develop the
capacities a free society would need.

This is not an attempt to build socialism inside capitalism as a
complete alternative — the charge Engels reasonably pressed against
Owen's New Harmony and Fourier's phalansteries. It is the construction
of the relationships, infrastructures and habits a transformed society
would require, while simultaneously contesting capital and the state.
Kropotkin theorised this as "collective withdrawal": the building of
political, social and economic networks beyond the reach of the state,
which would both sustain activists in periods of repression and bring
into being, prior to the state's collapse, the social relations capable
of supporting anarchist practices.[^5]

### Leninism as the more convincing utopianism

If utopian socialism names the project of imagining socialism as a
system to be designed and installed through the conversion of existing
power, then
<a href="Leninism" class="wikilink" title="Leninism">Leninism</a> fits
the definition rather more convincingly than anarchism does. The idea
that one can capture a state apparatus built by and for capital, wield
it through a vanguard party, and have it wither away on schedule is, in
retrospect, a remarkably idealist proposition about institutional
behaviour. The anarchist objection was always the more materialist one:
institutions shape the people who staff them, and an apparatus of
coercion will reproduce coercion regardless of whose hands hold it. That
this objection has been borne out by every twentieth-century experiment
in seizing the state for socialist ends is not, in itself, decisive —
but it is suggestive.

## Practice

Several historical anarchist projects have been read as
utopian-socialist in inspiration, though usually with little
justification beyond their experimental or communitarian character. The
<a href="Whiteway_Colony" class="wikilink"
title="Whiteway Colony">Whiteway Colony</a> in
<a href="Gloucestershire" class="wikilink" title="the Cotswolds">the
Cotswolds</a> (founded 1898), drawing on the Christian anarchism of
<a href="Leo_Tolstoy" class="wikilink" title="Leo Tolstoy">Leo
Tolstoy</a>, is sometimes grouped with the Owenite and Fourierist
colonies of the previous century. So is the
<a href="Life_and_Labor_Commune" class="wikilink"
title="Life and Labor Commune">Life and Labor Commune</a> in Soviet
Russia. The agrarian and industrial collectives of the
<a href="Spanish_Revolution" class="wikilink"
title="Spanish Revolution">Spanish Revolution</a> in Aragon and
Catalonia — documented by
<a href="Sam_Dolgoff" class="wikilink" title="Sam Dolgoff">Sam
Dolgoff</a> and others — are a different matter entirely: not
demonstration projects offered to the propertied for inspection, but
expropriations carried out by workers and peasants in the course of a
revolution against capital and state alike.[^6] The distinction matters.
A community that withdraws to model a better life for onlookers is doing
one thing; a community that seizes the means of production in open
conflict with the existing order is doing something else.

## Criticism

Anarchist criticism of utopian socialism focuses on three points. The
first concerns agency: the utopians sought a transformation they
expected the powerful to deliver, or at least permit, once persuaded.
This misreads how power behaves, and reduces the dispossessed to
beneficiaries rather than agents. The second concerns method: the
elaboration of detailed plans for the future tends, whatever the
planner's intentions, towards the authority of the plan. The third
concerns the relation between present and future. A community built as a
demonstration is not the same as a community built as part of a
struggle; the former depends on the indulgence of those it is meant to
convert, while the latter develops the capacities required to defeat
them.

These criticisms do not extend to the imaginative literature of
socialism, which anarchists have produced abundantly and read with
pleasure. They do not extend to the impulse to picture a free society,
which is integral to wanting one. They extend to the substitution of
picture for struggle, and to the address of that picture to those whose
power it would have to abolish.
