# Liberalism

Liberalism is the dominant political ideology of the capitalist world,
organised around a cluster of commitments to individual rights,
representative government, private property, market exchange, the rule
of law and formal legal equality. In its self-presentation it stands for
freedom, tolerance and reasoned debate. In practice, as anarchists have
argued since the nineteenth century, it has functioned as the political
form of
<a href="capitalism" class="wikilink" title="capitalism">capitalism</a>
and the <a href="state" class="wikilink" title="state">state</a>,
administering hierarchies of class, race and empire while disclaiming
responsibility for them.

Anarchists share with early liberals a hostility to arbitrary power,
inherited privilege and clerical authority, and acknowledge that
liberalism's radical wing once overlapped substantially with the early
socialist and workers' movements.[^1] What divides anarchism from
liberalism is not the value of liberty but the analysis of where
unfreedom comes from. Liberals locate it chiefly in the overreaching
state and ground their politics in reform of that state; anarchists
locate it in hierarchy as such — in the wage system, the property
regime, the police, the school, the family, the colony — and reject the
state as a vehicle for emancipation.

# Origins

Liberalism emerged in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries as a
revolutionary doctrine against absolutism, established religion and the
landed aristocracy. Its early classical form was, in
<a href="Kevin_Carson" class="wikilink" title="Carson">Carson</a>'s
phrase, a revolutionary ideology that declared war on the most
entrenched class interests of its day.[^2] Even mainstream figures such
as Adam Smith, James Mill and David Ricardo showed considerable
hostility to the landed oligarchy and to the politically connected
mercantilists who dominated Britain in the early nineteenth century. A
radical wing represented by Thomas Hodgskin went further, treating the
emerging capitalist order as a bastard fusion of partially free markets
with the old feudal class system, in which industrial capitalists were
amalgamating with the landed aristocracy to form a new ruling class.[^3]

This radical wing of classical liberalism overlapped heavily with the
early socialist movement at a time when a substantial part of the
workers' movement still aimed at abolishing the special privileges of
landlords and capitalists and building an economy based on workers'
cooperatives. It is from this overlapping zone that the American
individualist anarchists Josiah Warren, Lysander Spooner and Benjamin
Tucker drew, alongside Pierre-Joseph Proudhon's mutualism in
France.[^4][^5]

The decisive shift came around the middle of the nineteenth century,
when the industrial capitalists had supplanted the landed elites as the
dominant class in Britain. The main body of classical political economy
made the transition Marx called the move to "vulgar political economy",
taking up the role of hired ideological prizefighters for capitalist
interests.[^6] The contrast between the early and late Herbert Spencer
illustrates the change: the early Spencer, a disciple of Hodgskin,
attacked the artificial property rights of the landed elites and
regarded their rents as a species of taxation; the later Spencer, as
Benjamin Tucker observed, demonstrated the evils of laws ostensibly
passed to protect labour while never calling attention to the laws
creating privilege and sustaining monopoly.[^7]

# Tendencies

## Classical liberalism

Classical liberalism describes the strand that dominated nineteenth
century European politics. It argues for a limited "night-watchman"
state whose role is to guarantee social order and the operation of
laissez-faire exchange, leaving the individual the negative freedom to
act unmolested by government.[^8] Classical liberals accepted, and often
celebrated, the inequalities of outcome thrown up by markets, holding
that equality of opportunity and meritocracy were possible within an
unequal society. They were also traditionally suspicious of universal
suffrage, fearing a tyranny of the majority that might restrict the
liberty of the propertied.[^9]

Anarchists reject this position not because they oppose individual
liberty but because they regard the classical liberal account of
property and markets as already secretly statist. The "free market"
defended by classical liberals was in practice a market structured by
enclosure, by monopolies on land and credit, by tariffs and patents, and
by the legal infrastructure of wage labour — every one of them a
creation of the state in the service of capital. Liberty in such a
market is the liberty of those who already have property.

## Social liberalism

Social or "new" liberalism emerged in the late nineteenth and early
twentieth centuries, accepting a more interventionist state aimed at
reducing poverty and inequality and thereby promoting the positive
freedom of individuals to develop their capacities.[^10] It is the
direct ancestor of twentieth century welfare-state politics in Britain
and the United States and of the Progressive movement in particular.

Anarchists see in social liberalism not the humanisation of capitalism
but its stabilisation. The welfare state, as
<a href="Frances_Fox_Piven" class="wikilink"
title="Frances Fox Piven">Frances Fox Piven</a> and Richard Cloward
argued, was created precisely to prevent the working class from
organising to keep the product of its own labour and to control its
work, with its major expansions under FDR and LBJ spurred by drastic
increases in social disorder.[^11] Working class self-organisation —
friendly societies, mutual aid funds, penny-a-week schools, militant
syndicalist unions, the radical labour press — was already supplying
every function the "progressive" welfare state would later claim as its
own, but organised from the bottom up rather than handed down from on
high.[^12] The historical task of social liberalism, anarchists argue,
was to replace these autonomous working-class institutions with
paternalist substitutes administered by a new managerial class of social
workers, planners, technocrats and "helping professionals".[^13]

## Progressivism and the rule of experts

Twentieth century liberalism, in its Progressive and Fabian forms, has
been less a defence of liberty than an ideology of social control. Its
primary base of support was the New Class of social engineers and
credentialed professionals who saw themselves as appointed to manage the
lower orders for their own good.[^14] Herbert Croly's *The Promise of
American Life* sought, in his own phrase, "Jeffersonian ends with
Hamiltonian means", celebrating experts and intellectuals in almost
messianic terms; Walter Lippmann argued openly that the Great Society
could not be governed by men who thought deductively about rights and
wrongs but only by the technic of experts.[^15]

The Fabian variant in Britain was franker still. H. G. Wells favoured
minimum housing standards designed not to guarantee subsistence to poor
families but to end the availability of cheap housing on which the
destitute survived, and worried about perpetuating "the educationally
and technically unadaptable elements in the population". Sidney and
Beatrice Webb proposed relief conditioned on "treatment and disciplinary
supervision", with local councils determining who was "mentally
defective or an excessive drinker" and sending the uncooperative to
"Reformatory Detention Colonies".[^16] Hilaire Belloc, watching this
current take shape, described its endpoint as the "servile state" — a
society in which employers would be legally compelled to provide a
minimum of comfort to their workers in return for guaranteed profits,
and in which legally defined status would replace the right of free
contract.[^17]

The same logic appeared in the workplace as Taylorism: the deskilling of
the worker, the upward transfer of expertise to salaried engineers, and
the transformation of labour into rote repetition that engaged the
mental faculties as little as possible. It appeared in the school as the
manufacture of punctual, literate, orderly and compliant material for
the factory floor.[^18] Across all these sites the progressive project
was the same: to alienate the ordinary person from her own common sense
and rob her of the capacity to control the things that affect her daily
life.

## Neoliberalism

From the late 1970s onwards a revived classical liberalism returned to
dominance under names such as neoliberalism, the New Right and
Thatcherism, reasserting the priority of markets, deregulation and the
dismantling of the postwar welfare settlement.[^19] Anarchists treat
neoliberalism not as a departure from liberal principles but as one of
their honest expressions: the open subordination of public provision to
private capital, of citizenship to consumer choice, and of common life
to the discipline of the wage. The simultaneous expansion of the
carceral state, the security apparatus and the border regime confirms
the anarchist reading that liberalism's commitment to small government
applies only to government's redistributive functions, never to its
repressive ones.

# Liberalism and the state

Liberals differ amongst themselves over how large the state should be,
but converge on the thought that the state can in principle be made an
instrument of freedom — neutral in classical accounts, an arbiter of
competing pressure groups in pluralist accounts, an engine of positive
liberty in social-liberal accounts.[^20] Anarchists reject all three
pictures. The state is not a neutral container for politics; it is
itself a hierarchy, with its own interests in self-perpetuation, its own
ruling personnel and its own attachment to capital.

<a href="Leo_Tolstoy" class="wikilink" title="Tolstoy">Tolstoy</a> put
the practical point sharply in his 1896 letter to Russian liberals. A
government that holds in its grasp the army, the administration, the
church, the schools and the police, and frames the laws on the basis of
which liberals propose to resist it, will never let people who submit to
it and act under its guidance do anything that will undermine its
authority.[^21] Such a state will sanction pseudo-educational
establishments, sanctioned publications and toothless committees so long
as these serve its purpose; it will close them the moment they begin to
cure the blindness on which its power rests. The gradual conquest of
rights through engagement with state institutions is, on this view, a
self-deception that suits the government admirably and that the
government is therefore even ready to encourage.[^22]

Tolstoy's further point bears repeating because anarchists have
generally agreed with it. The participation of enlightened, good and
honest people in the affairs of government gives the government a moral
authority it would not otherwise possess. If the state were composed
entirely of the coarse element — the men of violence, self-seekers and
flatterers who form its core — it could not continue to exist. It is the
liberal reformer, in good faith, who supplies the prestige.[^23]

The same dynamic operates internationally. The liberal order that
defined itself through its opposition to the genocides of the twentieth
century has, as Ian Alan Paul has written of the destruction of Gaza,
cooperated to facilitate genocide in the twenty-first. Alliances remain
firm, logistical supports stay online, trade routes flow, the
international system survives, while an entire people is buried beneath
burning debris.[^24] The liberal order takes the moral high ground in a
world where rubble accumulates and graves are dug everywhere below; it
offers breathing room for regret and remorse in a world where machines
of mass death asphyxiate ever more lives.[^25] For every sanctified
constitution, a detention camp that will never close; for every promised
equality, an economy imposing its cruel hierarchies upon every area of
life; for each civic norm, a mob of police marching through the streets
drunk on power.[^26]

# Liberalism and revolt

A characteristic operation of contemporary liberalism is the
recuperation of revolt. Liberalism sees rebellion as something that was
necessary and needed in the past but always too extreme for the
present.[^27] It celebrates revolt as memory and pacifies it in
actuality, transforming insurgent movements into images, archived
cinders, museum exhibits and marketing material. The chant "the whole
world is watching" at demonstrations as people are dragged into police
vans shows the degree to which would-be insurgents have already learned
to embrace themselves as images, treating recognition by the powerful as
the horizon of their politics.[^28]

This logic — the subordination of resistance to its appearance as
representation — has been described by anarchist writers and organisers
as the operation of liberalism within movements themselves. Critical
resistance and uprising are repeatedly dragged back to compliance, with
permits, under the rhetoric of being peaceful or nonviolent.[^29]
Movement managers seek to make the movement mainstream-popular, inviting
celebrities and business leaders forward while pushing out the radical
elements that released the pressure valves to begin with. Working with
the city and police legitimises those avenues and makes it easier for
the police to divide and attack groups that act without state
permission.[^30] Demands for police reform crowd out conversation about
abolishing the infrastructure that makes racial oppression profitable,
and once the movement is focused on policy change, containment is
practically complete.[^31]

Behind this recuperation sits a third operation: counterinsurgency. When
liberal authorities enter into dialogue with the so-called
representatives of a revolt, their goal is to turn parts of the revolt
against itself, sharpening divisions between the saved and the damned,
the voices of reason and the cries of madness, the blessed protestor and
the cursed rioter.[^32] Before sending in the police, it is often
helpful to recruit new officers from within the movement — protestors
who have chosen to negotiate, agree to concessions and ultimately
cooperate with their own repression.[^33]

# Anarchist diagnoses

Anarchists, marxists and conservatives all use "liberal" as a slur, and
mean wildly different things by it.[^34] William Gillis has argued that
the marxist and conservative uses share a common picture in which
liberals are cowards in the war against their own conscience, people
without the stomach for the violence that needs to be done. The
anarchist diagnosis is the opposite: liberals are intellectual and
ethical cowards, halfassed in their analyses and strategies, settling
for the most tepid of desires.[^35] Liberals embrace state power and
other problematic means to achieve ameliorations in the short term at
the cost of future victories; they cut corners and look for quick fixes
on a global scale.[^36] They cede grassroots accountability to
centralised regulators that are promptly captured by the industries they
were meant to oversee; they trade wildcat unionism for bureaucratic
unions that function as a second wing of capital; they accept dependence
on employers and the state for health, education and welfare rather than
do the harder work of rebuilding cooperatives and consumer
organisations.[^37]

On this account marxism and conservatism are themselves variants of
liberalism — intensifications of its shortsightedness rather than
alternatives to it. The simplistic narratives of war, the embrace of
dictatorship, the love of centralisation and vertically structured
apparatuses of control all share liberalism's preference for immediate,
simplistic solutions over the patient work of dismantling
hierarchy.[^38]

# Anarchism and the liberal inheritance

The relation between anarchism and liberalism is not one of pure
opposition. Several of the most important figures in nineteenth century
anarchist thought drew openly on the radical wing of classical
liberalism. Josiah Warren, Lysander Spooner and Benjamin Tucker built an
individualist anarchism that retained the classical political
economists' attention to the difference between earned and unearned
income, to the privileges sustained by state-enforced monopolies in
land, money, tariffs and patents, and to the labour theory of
value.[^39][^40] Proudhon's mutualism, in which workers would own the
limited property necessary for their independence and exchange goods and
services on the basis of their costs of production, also retained
elements that are recognisably liberal in their attention to individual
autonomy and free association, while rejecting wage labour, profit and
the state.[^41]

Anarchist communism, in the work of
<a href="Peter_Kropotkin" class="wikilink"
title="Kropotkin">Kropotkin</a> and
<a href="Mikhail_Bakunin" class="wikilink" title="Bakunin">Bakunin</a>
and the individualism of William Godwin, departs further from
liberalism. Where liberals accept inequality of outcome as natural and
even desirable, these traditions argue that the liberal goal of
individual freedom is unattainable in a grossly unequal society, since
only a high degree of material equality gives all individuals access to
the resources needed to develop their capacities.[^42] Liberty, on this
view, is not a property of an individual abstracted from social
conditions but the concrete capacity to act, learn, organise and refuse
— a capacity that wage labour and propertyless dependence systematically
destroy.

The remaining inheritance from liberalism within anarchism is therefore
neither the state nor the market but the older insistence on the dignity
of the individual, the suspicion of inherited authority and the refusal
to grant any institution the right to act in one's name without consent.
Anarchism takes these commitments more seriously than liberalism does,
because it follows them all the way down.

# Criticism

Anarchist criticism of liberalism converges on several recurring points.

Liberalism mistakes the location of unfreedom. By focusing on the state
as the principal threat to liberty and on the market as the principal
sphere of free choice, it conceals the way wage labour, landlordism,
patriarchy, white supremacy and colonialism produce daily compulsion
that has nothing to do with formal law.[^43][^44]

Liberalism trades autonomy for protection. The welfare state, the
regulatory apparatus and the credentialed professions all involve
handing over functions that working-class communities once performed for
themselves to institutions that infantilise their clients and discipline
their behaviour.[^45]

Liberalism cannot keep its own promises. The same liberal orders that
proclaim universal human rights conduct or underwrite wars, sieges and
genocides; the same constitutions that guarantee speech and assembly
preside over surveillance, militarised policing and mass
incarceration.[^46] This is not hypocrisy in the ordinary sense but the
structural pattern of a politics whose function is to manage the
contradictions of capitalism, not to resolve them.

Liberalism domesticates revolt. By offering recognition in exchange for
moderation, it converts living movements into archived memories and
replaces struggle with representation.[^47][^48]

Liberalism corrupts those who try to work within it. Tolstoy's warning
remains pointed: to obtain the rights of a member of parliament one has
to abandon part of one's rights as a human being, and having abandoned
part of one's rights as a human being there is no longer any fixed point
of leverage.[^49] Enlightened reformers, drawn into government service
and the press, lend it the moral authority it could not generate on its
own and find themselves, by small compromises, the humble servants and
supporters of the very order against which they set out to fight.[^50]

What follows for anarchists is not a retreat from public life but a
different practice within it: building the institutions of
<a href="Mutual_Aid" class="wikilink" title="mutual aid">mutual aid</a>,
cooperative production, federated assembly and prefigurative autonomy
that liberalism has spent two centuries trying to render unthinkable.
The aim is not to capture the liberal state or to reform it but to make
it superfluous.
